About the Project

Reset Attention explores digital overload as a social, psychological and cultural problem.

Mission

We help people reclaim focus.

Every day people face endless notifications, social media feeds, breaking news, short videos and digital pressure. Our goal is to help young people understand how technology affects their focus, motivation, mental health and lifestyle.

Format

Independent digital magazine.

The website includes articles, videos, podcast recommendations, interactive polls, social media links and communication with the audience through Telegram.

Full Articles

All texts are ready for the English-language media website.

Attention

Why Your Attention Is Disappearing

Your attention is not gone — it is being hijacked. Every notification, scroll and recommendation is designed to pull you away from what matters. In the attention economy, focus becomes a product, and platforms compete for every second of your time.

The first step is awareness: turn off unnecessary notifications, create screen-free zones and stop treating boredom as a problem. Sometimes silence is not empty — it is recovery.

Dopamine

Dopamine and the Endless Scroll

Social media gives the brain quick rewards: likes, messages, short videos and novelty. These dopamine spikes make scrolling feel exciting, but they can also make deep focus feel boring.

The goal is not to eliminate dopamine. The goal is to reduce uncontrolled stimulation and learn to get motivation from effort, learning, movement and meaningful progress.

Health

Move Your Body. Sharpen Your Mind.

Exercise is not only fitness. Movement supports mood, motivation and attention. A walk, short workout or stretching break can reduce mental fatigue and make it easier to return to deep focus.

  • Move before long study sessions.
  • Use walks as attention-reset breaks.
  • Choose consistency over intensity.
Habits

How to Take Your Focus Back

Reclaiming attention does not mean rejecting technology. It means designing better boundaries. Focus grows when you reduce interruptions and practice single-tasking.

  • Start the day without your phone.
  • Work in 25–50 minute focus blocks.
  • Keep one screen and one task at a time.
  • Protect boredom — it helps creativity return.
